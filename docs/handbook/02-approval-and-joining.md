# Approval, and the join link

Approval is a decision by a person, not a switch flipped by software.
When the founder approves your request, the product provisions two
things: a place for you to work, and a personal invitation to it.

## What approval actually creates

- If your firm is new to Mandate, approval creates your organisation —
  a private workspace no other firm can see — and issues you a staff
  invitation as its **admin**. You own your team from day one.
- If your firm already runs on Mandate, approval issues you an
  invitation into the existing organisation at whatever role its
  situation calls for.

Either way, the artefact you receive is a single **join link**. The
product emails nothing; the founder sends the link to you personally,
at the address from your request.

## The join contract

The invite is the approval. Opening the link shows you exactly what you
are walking into: the organisation's name, who invited you, the role
you will hold, and the email the seat is for. There is no separate
"pending" state and no second review — nobody's account exists until
they set their own password at the link.

- The link is **personal and single-use**, and it expires **fourteen
  days** after issue.
- You choose a password (minimum length, mixed case, a digit and a
  symbol), and clicking the link is itself what confirmed your email.
- The moment your password is set you are signed in, landing on your
  desk — active, in your organisation, with your role already in place.

A dead link — expired, withdrawn, or already used — says only "This
link no longer works" and tells you to ask for a fresh one. It
deliberately does not reveal which kind of dead it is.
