## 2. The seven dimensions

### 2.1 Technical readiness — 🟢 **GO**

The strongest dimension and not the constraint. Verified today: **1,571 tests pass**,
`tsc --noEmit` clean, `eslint` clean, `next build` succeeds — with 40 uncommitted files
applied. Production is live and healthy (`200 {db:ok, auth:ok, cron:ok}`), last deployed 6
days ago. 159 migrations plus 160 applied today. Authorization is enforced in three
independent layers with the real boundary in Postgres, and every human write policy sampled
is role-aware.

Two caveats that do not change the verdict:
- 23 of 37 AI capabilities have never executed in production, so each first real use is also
  its first integration test
- 40 files of uncommitted UI work (the native-`<select>` replacement, 60 controls across 38
  files) are green but **behaviourally unverified** — the accompanying test is a source-tree
  guard that renders nothing

### 2.2 AI-output quality — 🔴 **NO-GO as a validated claim** · 🟡 acceptable for a pilot

**This is unmeasured, and that must not be papered over.**

- The judgment harness (`evals/judgment-harness/`) was **built and has never been run** — no
  `output/` directory exists
- The only benchmark result file is dated **2026-08-25**, and covers 6 of 37 capabilities
- 31 capabilities have no eval of any kind
- Measured production telemetry: 76 calls, **all `outcome = ok`** — so the structured-output
  discipline is holding, but the escalation, refusal and schema-failure paths have never
  fired in production
- **Zero cache hits ever recorded**, while two capabilities account for 52% of input tokens
- Latency is a product problem: `generate_interview_plan` averages **114.6s**,
  `run_candidate_research` **137.4s**, both behind a click with no streaming

**What is strong:** the *constraints* on AI output are verified in code — closed schemas on
all 38 model-calling paths, recursive stripping of verdict vocabulary before persistence,
approval gates and immutability enforced by database triggers, and an adversarial
verification pass on the evaluation seam. The guardrails are real; the accuracy is unknown.

**Pilot-acceptable because** the subscription terms disclaim accuracy honestly, every output
requires human review, and the pilot's explicit purpose is to measure this. **Not
acceptable** as a marketed quality claim.

### 2.3 Privacy and contractual readiness — 🔴 **NO-GO** → 🟡 after legal review

**No legal page of any kind is live.** The marketing footer links to three things:
`/handbook`, `/request-access`, `/status`. There is no terms page, privacy notice, DPA,
subprocessor list or cookie disclosure — for a product that processes candidate personal
data obtained without the candidate's involvement.

**Closed today:** the factual substrate. Twelve annexes covering data categories, sources,
flows and recipients, controller/processor roles activity-by-activity, hosting and transfers,
vendors and subprocessors, verified AI retention and training settings, retention/deletion/
backups/logs, access controls and incident handling, candidate notices and rights workflows,
the AI/human-review boundary, and the cookie analysis. Plus five labelled review drafts.

**Findings that matter:**
- **Cookie consent is very likely not required** — inspected rather than assumed. Four
  cookie-like items total: Supabase auth session, two first-party preference cookies, and
  Turnstile, which Cloudflare states *"does not use any cookies"*. **Zero analytics,
  advertising or tracking technology anywhere.** A disclosure is needed; a banner is not
- **Transfers are out of scope** — client, candidates and storage are all US (`us-east-1`).
  No SCCs, no IDTA, no TRA. **Conditional on the US-only scope, which nothing in the product
  enforces** — one EU candidate re-opens the whole analysis
- **No retention mechanism exists at all.** Verified across all 159 migrations: no TTL, no
  purge, no automatic deletion anywhere. Data persists while the account exists. The notices
  must say so or a control must be built
- **Deletion is suppression plus human resolution**, not hard deletion. Accurately described
  in the drafts; a lawyer must confirm sufficiency
- **Incident response does not exist** — so the DPA's breach clause is deliberately blank
- **The subprocessor list cannot be published** while two production credentials are
  unexplained

**Remaining:** qualified legal review (nothing substitutes), 9 blocking items in Annex M.1.

### 2.4 Infrastructure and recovery — 🔴 **NO-GO** → 🟢 for ~$25/month

**The most serious single finding in the whole readiness review, and the cheapest to fix.**

Verified live: the Supabase organisation is on **`free`**. On Free there are **no backups of
any kind**, 1-day log retention, and projects pause after a week idle. **If the database were
lost today there is no restore point.**

And the fact that survives any upgrade — verified against Supabase's own documentation:

> *"Database backups do not include objects you store via the Storage API."*

**No plan — Free, Pro, Team or Enterprise — backs up the `cvs` bucket.** Candidate CV files,
the agency's primary working material, have no backup path that can be purchased.

Also verified, and worth recording because it is counter-intuitive: **Pro at $25/month
includes no uptime SLA.** SLAs first appear on Team at $599/month. PITR is a separate
$100/month add-on that *replaces* daily backups and requires a compute add-on.

**Approved in principle (founder, 2026-10-07):** Pro + independent file backup. 24-hour RPO,
no PITR — correctly judged unnecessary at this stage. **Not yet implemented.** Three steps
are blocking: upgrade, build the file-backup step, rehearse one restore.

**A separate recovery blocker surfaced:** `001_core_schema.sql` is 0 bytes, so the repository
**cannot rebuild the database from scratch**. One `pg_dump --schema-only` committed as a
reference snapshot closes it.

### 2.5 Payment arrangement and entitlement correctness — 🟡 **GO for pilot** · 🔴 **NO-GO for self-service**

Payment collection and entitlement enforcement are separate requirements, and both are
absent: no Stripe, no provider code, and no plan/seat/quota concept in 72 tables.

- **Pilot: GO.** Manual invoicing against a signed agreement is appropriate and honest at
  n=1. Scope lives in Schedule A, enforced by it being one known customer
- **Self-service: NO-GO.** A published tier cannot be sold without a way to charge for it or
  a way to enforce it

**Sequencing recommendation: entitlements before billing.** Entitlements make the tier
structure *true*; billing makes it *collectable*. Billing first would charge for differences
the product does not enforce — the current pricing page's problem with a payment rail
attached.

### 2.6 Accuracy of the customer-facing offer — 🔴 **NO-GO until corrected**

All 21 published claims audited against implementation and production telemetry:

| | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
🔴 Inaccurate / misleading | **2** | **"Global Executive Network"** — the feature is org-scoped (`PK (organization_id, identity_key)`, RLS on org); it is the agency's *own* pool folded by person, not a shared database, and it is listed as an Agency exclusive. **"Custom skills + agents"** — custom agents do not exist |
🟠 Unsupportable | **2** | **"SLA"** (no plan provides one, nothing measures uptime); "Dedicated success partner" (2 humans) |
🟡 Undefined | **2** | **"30-day evaluation history"** (zero implementation); "Unlimited" |
🔵 Real but unenforced | **5** | seats, active searches, and three tier gates |
🟢 Accurate and verified | **4** | append-only audit trail, immutable approved records, calibration history + restore, versioned success profiles |
⚪ Built, never used | **3** | HM Portal, Triangulation, the EI chain |

**"30-day evaluation history" carries a specific trap.** The obvious implementation —
delete evaluations after 30 days — would build a data-destroying job to enforce a price tier
and destroy the agency's own work product. **Rejected.** Recommendation: remove the line now;
later, if tier differentiation needs it, implement as a *visibility window* over data that is
always retained.

Corrected copy is prepared for review. **`pricing.ts` is unchanged** — the commercial offer
is the founder's to alter.

## 3. The conditional-GO gate for the pilot

Seven conditions. None is large; together they are roughly two weeks and $25/month.

| # | Condition | Owner | Blocking because |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Supabase Pro + spend cap on** | Founder | No backups exist today |
| 2 | **File backup for the three buckets**, in the existing cron | Me, on approval | No plan covers the CV bucket |
| 3 | **One rehearsed restore**, elapsed time recorded | Both | An untested backup is a hypothesis; the RTO is otherwise a guess |
| 4 | **Legal pack reviewed by a lawyer** and the notices published | Founder + lawyer | No legal page is live |
| 5 | **Incident-response process written** | Founder | Cannot sign a breach commitment without one |
| 6 | **Service-role key rotated**; **password floor raised to 12 + four classes** | Founder | Known exposure; app policy currently bypassable |
| 7 | **Corrected pricing copy applied** | Founder | Two misleading claims, one unsupportable |

**Strongly recommended alongside (cheap, not strictly blocking):** `SENTRY_AUTH_TOKEN`
(production traces are minified) · an external uptime monitor on `/api/health` · an Anthropic
budget alert · the `pg_dump --schema-only` reference snapshot · verify and commit the
uncommitted select work after a 1440/390 and accessibility pass.

## 4. What this verdict explicitly does not say

- It does **not** say the product is ready for small, mid-market or enterprise customers.
  Each is a separate NO-GO with its own reasons
- It does **not** say the AI output is accurate. It says the guardrails are verified and the
  accuracy is unmeasured
- It does **not** certify compliance with any law. It assembles facts for a lawyer
- It does **not** treat Executive Intelligence as enterprise readiness
- It does **not** claim one passing pilot would generalise. Section 4.5 of the offer document
  defines eight success criteria; **meeting them unlocks Stage 2 work, not Stage 2 sales**

## 5. One-line summary

**The engineering is ready; the wrapper is not.** Seven conditions and about $25 a month
stand between Mandate and a defensible first engagement — and the single most urgent of them
is that a client's candidate data would currently be held on infrastructure with no backups
at all.
