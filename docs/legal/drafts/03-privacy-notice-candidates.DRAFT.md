## {{AGENCY_NAME}} — How we handle information about candidates

**Last updated: {{DATE}}**

### 1. Why you are reading this

{{AGENCY_NAME}} is an executive search and recruitment firm. We hold information about you
because we are considering you for a role, or because we think you may be relevant to roles
in the future.

**You may not have given us this information yourself.** Much of executive search works by
identifying people who are not actively looking. If that is how we came to hold your
details, this notice explains what we hold, where it came from, and what you can do about
it. If you applied to us directly, this notice still applies to you.

We use a platform called Mandate to manage this work. Mandate processes your information on
our instructions and does not use it for its own purposes.
`[DECISION: NOTICE-OWNER — under model (b) this paragraph points to Mandate's own notice]`

### 2. What we hold about you

Not every item applies to every person. Depending on how you came into our records, we may
hold:

- **Who you are** — your name, and the email address, phone number and general location we
  have for you
- **Your professional background** — your current and previous job titles and employers,
  and how long you have worked in your field
- **Links to your public professional presence** — for example a LinkedIn, GitHub or
  personal website address, and a note of where we found you
- **Your CV or résumé**, if we have one — both the original file and the text we extract
  from it so it can be searched and assessed
- **Our own notes** — a recruiter's written assessment of your fit for a role, notes from
  conversations with you, and where you are in a search process
- **Call recordings and transcripts**, but **only where you were told the call was being
  recorded and agreed to it.** Our system requires that confirmation before a recording can
  be attached
- **Our correspondence with you** — the emails we have sent you and your replies
- **Computer-assisted assessments** of your fit for a specific role — see section 6
- **A record of your preferences**, including if you have asked us not to contact you, asked
  us to delete your information, or withdrawn from a search. We keep a record of that
  request specifically so that we honour it and do not re-add you later

**Information you may have included yourself.** A CV is free text and some people choose to
mention things like health, nationality, or personal circumstances. If your CV contains
information like that, we will hold it because it is in the document you gave us — we do not
ask for it, we do not seek it out, and our systems are specifically built not to draw
conclusions from it (section 6).

### 3. Where we got it

- **From you** — if you applied, sent us your CV, or updated your own details through a link
  we sent you
- **From public professional sources** — for example a public LinkedIn profile, a company
  website, a conference listing, or a published article
- **From our own records** — if we have dealt with you before
- **From our clients** — occasionally an employer suggests someone they would like us to
  approach
- **From automated web research.** Our platform can search publicly available web sources
  and summarise what it finds about a person's professional background. This is drawn from
  information that is already public; it does not access anything private

### 4. What we use it for, and why we are allowed to

We use your information to assess whether you are a good fit for roles we are working on, to
contact you about those roles, to present you to our client if you are interested and we
think you are suitable, and to keep an accurate record of our dealings with you.

`[DECISION: US-BASIS — under the agreed US-only scope the CCPA framing is "purposes for
which we collect", and a lawful-basis analysis in the GDPR sense is not required. If the
scope ever includes EU/UK candidates, legitimate interests plus a balancing test is the
usual route for sourced candidates and must be documented here. Annex M.3.]`

**What we do not do:** we do not sell your information, and we do not share it for
advertising or any similar purpose.

### 5. Who else sees it

- **Our client**, where you are being considered for their role and we present you. We tell
  you before we do this
- **The hiring manager** for a specific search, through a secure link limited to that search
- **Our technology suppliers**, who process information on our behalf under contract and may
  not use it for their own purposes. These are: Supabase (database and file storage, hosted
  on Amazon Web Services in the United States), Vercel (application hosting), Anthropic (the
  AI service that performs analysis), Resend (email delivery), Sentry (error monitoring,
  which receives technical error reports from which personal details are stripped before
  they are sent), and Cloudflare (bot protection on our public web form, which sees the IP
  address of visitors)
- **Professional advisers or authorities**, where we are required by law

Your information is **stored in the United States**.

### 6. Computer-assisted assessment — what the technology does and does not do

We use AI to help us assess candidates. We want to be straightforward about this.

**What it does.** It reads your CV and extracts a structured summary. It scores your fit
against the criteria our client has set for a role, across named dimensions, and writes a
narrative summary of strengths, gaps and risks. It may draft an outreach message or a set of
interview questions. It can summarise publicly available web information about your
professional background.

**What it does not do.**

- **It does not decide anything.** Every output is reviewed by a person, who can edit or
  reject it. No decision about whether to approach you, present you, or reject you is made
  by software
- **It does not produce a hire or no-hire verdict.** Our platform is built so that it cannot
  — any verdict-like output is removed before it is saved
- **It does not label you psychologically** or make any assessment of mental health
- **It does not infer protected characteristics** — such as race, religion, disability, age,
  sexual orientation or political opinion — and is specifically built not to

**Being honest about the limits.** A score influences the person reading it, even though it
does not decide. And the accuracy of these assessments has not been independently validated
by a third party. If you think an assessment of you is wrong, you can tell us (section 7)
and a person will look at it.

### 7. What you can ask us to do

You can ask us to:

- **Tell you what we hold** about you
- **Correct it** if it is wrong or out of date
- **Delete it**
- **Stop contacting you** — we will keep a minimal record of that request, and only that, so
  we do not contact you again by mistake
- **Take you out of a particular search** while remaining in our records, or not

**How to ask.** `[DECISION: RIGHTS-INTAKE — a published email address and at least one other
method are needed. Today a candidate who holds a portal link can use it; a candidate who
does not has no route in. Annex J.3]`

**What happens when you ask us to delete.** We will stop using your information for
recruitment and place you on a suppression list. We keep the suppression record itself —
your name or email and the fact you objected — because that record is what stops you being
re-added when your details next appear from another source. Where we are able to delete the
rest, we will. There are limited situations where we must keep some information, for example
where we have a legal obligation to retain a record.
`[DECISION: DELETION-DESCRIPTION — this paragraph describes what the code does today:
suppression plus human resolution, with two recorded decline outcomes. It is accurate. A
lawyer must confirm it is a sufficient response to a CCPA deletion request, and whether the
decline reasons map to the statutory exceptions. Annex H.2]`

**How quickly.** `[DECISION: RESPONSE-TIME — CCPA sets statutory deadlines. No commitment is
made here until the founder confirms the operational process can meet it. Annex J.3]`

### 8. How long we keep it

`[DECISION: RETENTION — THE MOST IMPORTANT BLANK IN THIS DOCUMENT. The product currently has
no automatic deletion of any kind (Annex H.1 — verified across all 159 migrations). As
built, information is retained for as long as the agency's account exists. Three options:
(a) state that plainly and honestly; (b) commit to a retention period and build the control
to enforce it — this does not exist today; (c) commit to a periodic human review. Do not
state a schedule the system does not implement.]`

### 9. Security

Your information is held in access-controlled systems. CV files and call recordings are kept
in private storage that is not publicly reachable. Access is limited by role, and enforced by
the database itself rather than only by the application. Recruiters see only their own
organisation's records.

## Drafting notes for review — not part of the notice

1. **Section 6 is the section to get right.** It is accurate as written and deliberately
   concedes two things most vendors would omit: that a score influences a human even without
   deciding, and that accuracy is unvalidated. Both are true (Annex K.3). Softening them
   would make the notice a misrepresentation; the judgment harness has never been run.
2. **The consent claim in section 2 is real.** `candidate_notes.consent_confirmed` is a
   required boolean before audio can attach (Annex A.1) — this is a verified control, not
   aspiration.
3. **The suppression explanation in section 7 is unusual and worth keeping.** Telling a
   person *why* you retain their objection pre-empts the obvious complaint that you did not
   fully delete.
4. **Section 8 cannot ship blank.** Of all the markers in this pack, retention is the one a
   reviewer will object to first.
5. **No GDPR vocabulary is used.** Deliberate, per the agreed US-only scope. The Art. 14
   machinery in the product (Annex J.1) is retained and is what makes an EU version cheap
   later.
